# conclusion

Harvesting remains the manual stage at ArianeTech and across Singapore’s vertical farms, and gripping the leaves is the main source of crop damage. Through systematic ideation, a morphological chart and weighted scoring, the team selected a fork-lift harvester with a fixed waterjet, then refined it through four virtual iterations. Each iteration removed a weakness of the last: the flip and comb, the loose heads, and finally the drop into a chute. The final design, Harvester_v2, holds each head by the base of its stem in a split cup, gathers the leaves with S-curved tines, cuts a whole row with one jet and sets the heads down on the tray. Its simulated rate of about 1,350 heads per hour is 13–18 times the observed manual rate. The critical unknown is whether an affordable pump can cut the stems; Semester 2 will answer it by test, alongside safe cup force and an integrated trial with ArianeTech lettuce.
